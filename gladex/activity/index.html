Day 1

Activity 1 · with your buddy · about 40 minutes

Install and setup check

At the end: Claude Code runs on your laptop, with a chief of staff that has a name.

WHY · GWENEverything else in the two days runs on this. A setup problem found now takes 2 minutes. Found later, it eats an hour.
  1. Press the Windows key, type PowerShell, press Enter.A dark window with a blinking line.
  2. Copy this line, right-click inside PowerShell to paste, press Enter. 
    $env:CCI_SKILLS='hire,setup'; irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jtlgrowth/claude-code-installer/main/install.ps1 | iex
    Lines of text scrolling. If it asks Y/n, press Enter. If Windows asks for permission, click Yes.
  3. When it asks for the Workshop key, type the key from the whiteboard.It keeps installing. This takes a few minutes.
  4. When it finishes, close PowerShell. Open a new one.A fresh, empty window.
  5. Type claude and press Enter. Then type /login and finish in the browser.Claude Code waiting for your message.
  6. Type /setup. Answer the three questions: its name, how it talks, what it asks you first.Every reply now starts with that name.
  7. Check your buddy's laptop: installed, opens, logged in, named.Both laptops pass all four.

Done when: both laptops pass all four checks. First pair to shout "Hired!" wins the install race.

Stuck? "Running scripts is disabled": press Windows key, type cmd, paste the line below. "'claude' is not recognized": close every window, open a new one, or restart. Still stuck after 2 minutes: hand up.

powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -Command "$env:CCI_SKILLS='hire,setup'; irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jtlgrowth/claude-code-installer/main/install.ps1 | iex"
Activity 2 · 20 minutes

Hire your AI employee

At the end: one employee with one job, living in its own folder.

WHY · GWENA folder is its desk, so its files never mix with your other work. The seven questions are the same ones you ask a real new hire. Reading the job back catches the wrong job before it does the wrong work.
  1. In PowerShell, make your desk. Change marketing-hq to yours: posters-hq, hr-hq, audit-hq, ops-hq, qa-hq, it-hq. 
    mkdir marketing-hq
    Nothing. That is normal.
  2. Go inside it.
    cd marketing-hq
    The line now ends with your folder name.
  3. Type claude.Claude Code opens with your chief of staff's name.
  4. Type /hire.Question 1 of 7.
  5. Answer each question from your crib card. Short answers are fine.The next question, until all seven are done.
  6. It shows three files. Type yes.It says your employee is hired.
  7. Ask: "Read docs/team/[name].md to me."Its job in plain English. Wrong? Tell it what to change.

Done when: roster.json lists your employee, and its job description sounds right to you.

Activity 3 · 10 minutes · on paper

Write the brief for your one task

At the end: five lines that tell your employee exactly what to do.

WHY · GWENAI is only as good as your instructions. Vague brief, vague output. Five parts means it cannot guess what you meant.
  1. Goal: what done looks like, in one line."A weekly ads summary management can read in 2 minutes."
  2. Input: which file or folder it reads."Last week's exports in ads/."
  3. Output: the format and the filename."One table plus 3 moves, saved as ads/report.md."
  4. Check: how you will know it is right."Totals match the export."
  5. Never: what it must not do or guess."Never invent a number that is not in the file."
  6. Stuck? Paste this into Claude Code with your rough idea. 
    Here is my brief: [brief]
Rewrite it with five parts: Goal, Input, Output, Check, Never.
Ask me about anything missing before you start.
    It asks what is missing, then gives you a clean brief.

Data rule: no passport or ID numbers, card or bank details, salaries or passwords. Use "Client A" and sample data.

Activity 4 · 25 minutes

First shift: run it, check it, fix the brief once

At the end: a real file made by your employee, and a better second version.

WHY · GWENIt can be confidently wrong, and your name goes on the work. We fix the brief, not the answer, so the next run comes out right on its own.
  1. Paste your five-part brief.It starts reading your files.
  2. Wait. Do not type while it works. Press Esc only if it goes the wrong way.It lists what it is doing.
  3. It asks to create a file. Choose Yes.The file appears in your folder.
  4. Check 1, Source. Paste: 
    For every number in [file], tell me which file and which line it came from.
Mark anything you calculated or guessed.
    Each number with where it came from.
  5. Check 2, Sample. Check three lines yourself.Three right: read on. One wrong: fix the brief.
  6. Fix the brief and paste it again.A second version. Compare it with the first.
  7. Check 3, Sign. Post in the group chat: its name, the brief, the file it made.Done. That was its first shift.

Done when: your second output is better than your first, and you can say why.

Build its brain

At the end: a brain/ folder your employee reads before every task.

WHY · GWENWithout memory you repeat yourself forever. The brain is how it learns your desk once. Plain text files: no app to buy, nothing locked in.
  1. Open PowerShell. Go to your desk and start Claude. 
    cd marketing-hq
claude
    The same employee as yesterday.
  2. Paste the interview prompt. 
    Ask me 10 questions about my desk and my one task,
one at a time. Wait for my answer each time.
Then write company.md, desk.md, process.md and rules.md
in a folder called brain/. Use my words, not marketing words.
Then add to CLAUDE.md: read brain/ before every task.
    Question 1 of 10.
  3. Answer in your own words, one at a time.It waits for you after every question.
  4. Let it write the files. Choose Yes.Four files inside brain/.
  5. Add the safety rules. 
    Add these to brain/rules.md:
Never send anything outside without my approval.
Never quote a fare or a price I have not given you.
Never paste client IDs, cards or salaries.
Never guess a number. Say where it came from.
If unsure, stop and ask me.
    Your rules saved.
  6. Put three past outputs you were proud of in brain/examples.md. Sample data only.It now knows what good looks like.
  7. Run yesterday's brief again. Count the fixes, yesterday vs today.Fewer fixes today.

Done when: today's output needs fewer fixes than yesterday's, from the same brief.

Activity 6 · 45 minutes

Build your desk's screen

At the end: one screen for your one task, in your own look, with sample data.

WHY · GWENA screen makes your employee easy for the whole team to use. Decide the look once in DESIGN.md and every screen follows it. Pages that look AI-made get ignored.
  1. On paper: two fonts, four colors (background, text, accent, warning), and one thing you never want on a screen.Your choices, ready to paste.
  2. Paste with your brackets filled: 
    Create DESIGN.md for Gladex.
Fonts: [heading] + [body]
Colors: [background], [text], [accent], [warning]
Never: [one thing you hate]
Write it as rules my AI must follow on every page.
    A file called DESIGN.md.
  3. Paste: 
    Read DESIGN.md and brain/.
Build index.html: one screen for my one task, with sample data.
It must work on a phone first. Open it in my browser.
    Your screen in the browser.
  4. Make the browser narrow, or open it on your phone.The phone view. Check this first.
  5. Say what is off, round 1: 
    The spacing feels [crowded], the [title] is too [small], and
[the table] is hard to scan. Fix those, follow DESIGN.md, nothing else.
    It fixes only what you said.
  6. Do rounds 2 and 3.Better each time.
  7. Screenshot it and post in the group chat.Done. That is your desk's screen.

Activity 7 · 15 minutes · on paper

Your win card

At the end: the number that proves your employee is worth keeping.

WHY · GWENNobody adopts a tool. They adopt a win. A number is what your manager remembers.
  1. Write your one task."The weekly ads report."
  2. Hours a week before, and after (brief, check, sign).Example: 6 before, 1 after.
  3. Saved per week times 52.Example: 5 x 52 = 260 hours a year.
  4. Who checks it before it leaves your desk.A name. Usually yours.
  5. What it never does alone, and who else on your team could use it.Your guardrail and your next teammate.
