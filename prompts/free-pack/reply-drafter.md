# Reply drafter in your voice

Learns from three past replies you paste, then drafts three replies to a new message.

## The prompt

Paste this into Claude Code.

```
I want you to learn how I write and draft replies for me.

Ask me for two things first:
1. Three examples of real replies I have sent before (emails, chats, anything), pasted in full so you can see my actual voice.
2. The new message I need to reply to right now.

From the three examples, work out: how formal or casual I am, how long my replies usually run, whether I use exclamation points or emoji, and how I usually open and close a message. Do not ask me to describe my own voice, read it from the examples.

Then write a file called "voice-notes.md" with what you learned in plain bullet points, so you do not have to re-learn it next time I ask.

Then draft three replies to the new message: one safe and short, one warmer and longer, one that says no or pushes back if that fits the situation. Label each one clearly.

When you are done, tell me which of the three you would send and why.

To check it worked: read the three drafts out loud. If none of them sound like something you would actually type, tell Claude what is off (too stiff, too long, wrong greeting) and have it redo voice-notes.md before drafting again.
```
