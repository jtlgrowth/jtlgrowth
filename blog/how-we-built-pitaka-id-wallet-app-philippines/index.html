How we built Pitaka, an ID wallet app for the Philippines, and the skin bug our tests could not see

By Jamz Jamorol, founder of JTL Growth, Metro Manila, Philippines · 27 November 2026

Pitaka is an ID wallet for the iPhone, built by Venice, our AI operator, with me reviewing. Its looks (skins) taught us that a test lane covering one look does not protect twelve: when we finally shot every look, the screenshots showed four bugs that every green build had missed. The looks are in a build that is not on the App Store as of 2026-11-27. The listed version that day is 0.3.

What Pitaka is

A Filipino wallet is mostly IDs, the cards you need copies of for every loan, job or rental form. Pitaka keeps them on your iPhone. The home screen says it plainly under the title, how many IDs you hold and that they are on this iPhone only. It also helps with the paperwork side, such as sending a set of requirements as one PDF.

Venice, our AI operator, wrote and tested the app. I picked the look, tried it on my own phone and decided what went in. The App Store listing is named Pitaka: ID Wallet.

A name that was already taken

Our first hurdle was the name. On 2026-10-23, App Store Connect refused the app name Pitaka with a duplicate error: another developer's account owns it. The record is now Pitaka: ID Wallet, and the name under the icon on your home screen is still Pitaka, because the display name is a separate setting from the store name. Small thing, but it is the kind of problem you meet only when you try to ship.

The moment it went wrong

Pitaka grew looks, which are skins for the whole app. The free default is Black Metal, and there are 11 more, each with its own palette, card faces and layout. We were proud of how they looked in the mock-ups.

Then we ran a lane that shot every look on the screens that carry the most of a look. Every earlier build had been green. The screenshots told a different story.

On six of the looks the name on the member card had disappeared. On one look, list rows showed through the dock at the bottom. Wide mixed-case poster words had lost letters at the edges. On looks without a script typeface, the line saying how much was left until payday had vanished from under the amount.

None of it crashed anything. Every unit test passed. The bugs simply lived on screens our tests never looked at.

What we tried first, and why it did not hold

Our main screenshot lane was pinned to a single look, the free default. That was correct when the app had one look. When the skins arrived, the lane kept reporting green about Black Metal while eleven others went unchecked.

We had added checks as the looks grew. An earlier commit made the margins honest across all of them with a spacing check, and each rule had a planted failing case in its self-test. That helped, but it checked geometry, not whether the right text was readable and present on each look. The check itself still had to learn what a correct look is: it misread outline posters, tilted cards and one look's side rail.

The lesson we wrote down: a check that sees one configuration keeps passing while the others drift.

The real fix

Commit 02cd227fb fixed the four bugs at their causes, not their symptoms.

The vanishing member card name came from card text using the screen palette. It now uses the card face's own ink and a softer ink, and the contrast check covers the soft ink too. The rows showing through the dock came from a translucent tone laid over nothing, so it is now laid on the base colour first. The lost poster letters came from cropping every poster word, so now only all-caps faces are cropped. The missing payday line came from looks without a script face dropping it, so it is now drawn plain in the display face.

We also corrected the spacing check. Centred lines are not evidence of a margin, and a planted case in its self-test now proves the check says so. One look made of words on a plain ground opts out of the margin rule openly, instead of faking a pass.

The check that guards it now

A dedicated looks lane runs every look on the home, spending, cards, card, paywall and looks screens. Each look gets a content check and a spacing check, and Apple's accessibility audit runs on three of those screens for each look. The list of looks is read straight from the token tables, so a new look gets shot without anyone editing the script. The main lane stays pinned to one look on purpose and calls the looks lane.

The run that closed it, on 2026-11-14, was green: 134 unit tests, 70 of 70 main screenshot tiles, 84 of 84 looks tiles, and zero accessibility failures at the light and Large text sizes. We also added 14 Filipino strings for the Looks screen.

Two smaller catches about trust

An ID wallet has to be honest about where data lives. On 2026-11-11 we removed a line from the More apps sheet that said your data stays on your phone. It was true for Pitaka but not for our other apps, Hugas and Suki Cart, which sync to a server, so the line now says who made the apps instead. And the update notice now asks first: the privacy policy promises it will ask inside the app before checking the App Store, so the check runs only after you tap Allow.

What we would do differently

We would build the all-looks lane alongside the first skin, not after the layouts existed. Every bug was small once we could see it. The cost was in how late it became visible.

We would plant a failing case for each new rule from the start. A check you have never seen fail is a check you cannot fully trust. And we would read a green lane as a statement about what it covers, not about the app. Ours was telling the truth about one look the whole time.
